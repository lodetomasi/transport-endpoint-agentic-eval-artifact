\section{Distribution-free sensitivity for the pre-registered family}
\label{sec:appendice-permutazione}

The pre-registered test is a paired $t$ over 45 binaries, which assumes approximately normal paired
differences. On this metric they are not: pass-rate over five unit tests takes six values, 53\% of rows
sit at an extreme, and the paired difference of two such means is discrete with short tails. If a test
assuming no distribution reaches the same conclusion, the assumption was not carrying the result. For
paired differences the exact permutation test is the sign flip --- under the null the sign of each
binary's difference is exchangeable --- and with $2^{45}$ assignments we sample 50{,}000 at a declared
seed and report the Monte Carlo error of the $p$ itself, a sampled $p$ being an estimate.

\input{tables/permutazione}

\textbf{No test passes Holm under any of the three series}, so the family outcome does not depend on the
distributional assumption, and nothing pre-registered changes.

Two contrasts disagree by more than the Monte Carlo error, informatively: T7 moves from 0.21 to 0.50 and
T4 from 0.64 to 0.78, and both are the contrasts with the smallest observed variance, where the $t$
divides by a standard error the permutation test never uses. That the $t$ reports smaller $p$ exactly
where variance is smallest is the mechanism rather than a coincidence, and it supports the reading of
\S\ref{sec:results}: T7's narrow interval reflects how little that cell varies, not how much evidence it
carries. This analysis is a sensitivity
result and does not replace the frozen one: had the series diverged on the family outcome, the reportable
conclusion would be the pre-registered one with the divergence stated beside it.

\section{A run-level model, and what it changes}
\label{sec:appendice-glmm}

The pre-registered test operates on per-binary means. A reader may ask what a model at run level would
have found, and the released rows allow the answer to be computed rather than argued. A marginal binomial
model with cluster-robust errors --- GEE with exchangeable working correlation, clustered on the binary,
over each contrast's individual test outcomes --- gives $z{=}-2.43$ ($p{=}0.0152$) on T3, $z{=}-2.45$
($p{=}0.0142$) on T6, and $z{=}1.63$ ($p{=}0.1039$) on T5. That is the frozen test's reading: the same two
nominal significances, the same non-significance, and nothing surviving Holm at $m{=}10$. The run-level
structure changes no conclusion.

The same script also fits a variational binomial mixed model, and we report it as a caution rather than a
result: it returns $|z|$ between 5 and 17 on the same contrasts where the paired $t$ and the GEE both give
$|z|$ between 1.6 and 2.5, which is the signature of a variational approximation understating posterior
uncertainty, not of an effect the design missed. Both fits are exploratory, enter no family, and their
reading criterion was written into the script before the numbers existed.

\section{Provenance of the re-collection's concordance diagnostics}
\label{sec:appendice-provenienza}

\S\ref{sec:threats} grounds the re-collection's role in the apparatus: the shared-workspace mechanism is
absent there by construction. The four concordance diagnostics are a separate instrument, and this appendix
records their provenance so that a reader auditing the artifact finds the timings already stated rather
than having to reconstruct them. Every figure recomputes from the released rows.

Two readings follow. Relative to the re-collection, the thresholds were fixed while the batch had produced
one cell of sixteen from a single model, and six of the eight paired contrasts remained uncomputable even
at the latest of the three timestamps. Relative to the original collection, which had completed, they were
fixed with its outcome known. The four are accordingly used as \textbf{pre-specified concordance
diagnostics on a comparison}, and the choice of primary basis rests on the apparatus (\S\ref{sec:threats}),
where the contaminating mechanism is absent by construction.
