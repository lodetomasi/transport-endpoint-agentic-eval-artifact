\begin{abstract}
\textbf{A serving endpoint can remove a model from an evaluation before it produces a score, or destroy
its measurements from inside one, and we document eight mechanisms with the endpoint's message for each.}
They are not equally easy to find: four are returned to an ordinary single-request capability check, three
require a deliberately constructed conversational state, and one appears only when the model itself emits
the output that triggers it. Ordinary capability checks are therefore not sufficient, and a harness
certified healthy before collection can still lose a cell after the money is spent. Counted as
model-platform pairs, eight of the 21 in the roster we assembled never produced a score --- five of the 18
on the two platforms the design measures. The roster was assembled rather than sampled, so these are
existence proofs with a stated denominator and not an incidence rate. \textbf{The transport moves a number,
which is a bias. The endpoint deletes a row, which is a selection} --- though not uniformly: some removals
are access and lifecycle policies no pre-flight reaches, while others are collisions with a request
contract the vendor documents, which a reader of the API reference would have caught before collecting.

That distinction is what the paper measures: four models, two managed clouds, two transports, 45 held-out
reverse-engineering tasks, 5{,}809 trials. The principal hypotheses and the analysis plan were hash-frozen
before collection; two of the ten tests were implemented under a timestamped amendment after collection had
begun.

Second, temperature zero is not determinism: across the corpus, one model returns identical runs on
91--100\% of binaries and another on 20--24\%. That output distributions can differ between services at
fixed model identity is already established; what \S\ref{sec:results} adds is one case where the
difference reaches the \emph{stability of a downstream agentic score}.

Third, the confirmatory family resolves no effect: no test passes its Holm threshold. The observed standard
deviations span a factor of 23.7, so each contrast is reported with the precision implied by its own
observed dispersion --- what a design can separate is a property of the cell, not of the study.

What transfers is the checklist of Table~\ref{tab:checklist} and the variance-decomposition method, not
the coefficients. On the two contrasts with the largest between-task dispersion, the lever that binds is the number of
tasks and not the number of runs, which inverts the calibration this design was sized on --- so on a task family with
this much between-task heterogeneity, a borrowed calibration sends a budget to the axis that does not
bind.
\end{abstract}
