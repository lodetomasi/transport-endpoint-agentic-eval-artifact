\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why the census carries the weight}

The transport moves a number, which is a bias. The endpoint deletes a row, which is a selection. The two
also differ in what it takes to check them: the census is checked by re-issuing a probe, the contrasts by
re-running 5{,}809 trials. \textbf{Nothing in \S\ref{sec:census} is an estimate} --- an endpoint returned
that message or it did not, and the artifact carries the request and the configuration that produced it ---
while every number in \S\ref{sec:results} is a point estimate with an interval a larger study could move.

The distinction that matters for reading \S\ref{sec:results} is between a \emph{null} result and an
\emph{uninformative} one, and what separates them is stated per contrast rather than for the design: the
effect each contrast could have resolved given its own dispersion ranges over a factor of 23.7. A design
sized on a single band would have quoted one number for eight contrasts that differ by more than an order
of magnitude in what they could separate.


\subsection{What the null confirmatory family could resolve}

Ten pre-registered tests, none passing its threshold, and the sizing that produced them is locatable.
\S\ref{sec:rq6} measures the lever that binds on this apparatus at the number of tasks --- 0.5\%
run-to-run noise and a floor of 668 binaries on the contrast with the largest observed dispersion ---
against the 84\% and thirteen the design was sized on.

\textbf{The consequence for a designer is that the sizing axis is itself an empirical question, answerable
only on the apparatus that will be used.} A calibration inherited across apparatuses can send a budget to
repeated runs when what binds is task diversity, and no amount of care at design time substitutes for
measuring it. The rival account for the decomposition --- that the granularity of the metric produces the
spread on its own --- is measured rather than argued: \S\ref{sec:granularita} recomputes the decomposition
on the subsets where the metric is at neither floor nor ceiling.

\subsection{When to discard a batch, and when not to}

Two batches were invalidated and re-collected rather than patched, and the rule separating them from the
one kept is reusable. \textbf{Discard when the contaminating channel is systematic and asymmetric
between the compared arms; report and bound it otherwise.}

The larger invalidation --- 2{,}390 measurements --- meets that test: the algorithm's name reached the model
through three separate channels and the two arms encountered one at different rates, so left in place it
would have turned part of the transport effect into a measure of how often each transport stumbled onto the
answer. No downstream analysis separates a contaminant that correlates with the manipulated variable.

The shared working directory (\S\ref{sec:threats}) fails the same test in the other direction:
non-directional, thirty times in the original batch's 5{,}805 measurements, with no asymmetry between arms.
So it was bounded, probed, and re-collected on isolated workspaces rather than discarded. Both batches
remain in the artifact with their recomputation.

\subsection{What an evaluation should report}

What follows amends an existing checklist rather than proposing a new one: the community guidelines already
require the harness to be described once it goes beyond bare API calls~\citep{baltes2026guidelines}, and
each field below either sharpens that requirement or makes explicit something those guidelines leave
implicit. Each costs a line in a method section, and Table~\ref{tab:checklist} states them as fields
because a recommendation phrased as prose is one a reader agrees with and does not apply. The first two
carry the argument of \S\ref{sec:runtime}: a model name does not identify a measurable system, two
deployed services under one model identity differing in trajectory length, latency and reported context
volume.

\subsection{Seven propositions that should outlive this task}
\label{sec:proposizioni}

Within the scope \S\ref{sec:threats} bounds, seven propositions about how experiments of this kind behave,
each instantiated by this study rather than proved in general.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The transport belongs beside temperature in a method section.
  \item An endpoint can produce missing experimental units, whose cost \S\ref{sec:bound} bounds.
  \item Variance structure differed substantially across the contrasts of this study, so a single
        inherited calibration is unreliable across the cells of one design. What follows for a designer is
        an independent pilot calibration, or an internal re-estimation whose rule is specified in advance
        and read blind to the outcome --- not a recalibration decided once the first outcome-bearing cell
        is visible, which would reintroduce the design freedom the pre-registration removes.
  \item A pre-flight must exercise realistic conversational states: three of our eight mechanisms are
        invisible to a probe that sends one call per turn, and a fourth is reachable only during
        execution.
  \item Metric granularity limits what a variance decomposition can say. We report the floor and ceiling
        shares beside ours, recompute the decomposition on the subset where the metric is at neither
        (\S\ref{sec:granularita}), and did not size the tests per task.
  \item An incidence rate for pre-score removal requires a \emph{sampled} roster, not an assembled one.
        Ours is assembled --- the models two clouds happened to serve, plus those probed while building the
        sample --- so it yields the fraction inside itself, 8 of 21 pairs, and no rate beyond. The measure
        this study does not supply is a probe run against a roster drawn by a stated sampling rule, which
        would turn eight existence proofs into an estimate with an interval.
  \item Whether a variance component is specific to an apparatus is not decidable from one apparatus. Two
        collections of ours agree with each other, which is reproducibility and not transfer; the question
        needs two apparatuses whose calibrations are both verifiable, and the one we inherited was not.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
The artifact carries each with its argument.
