\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{A serving endpoint decides which models an evaluation can measure, and it decides before any score
exists.} Six mechanisms remove a model-platform pair outright and two destroy measurements from inside a
run that has already been paid for, each documented with the endpoint's own words and the configuration
that produced it. Eight of the 21 pairs attempted here never produced a score.

\textbf{They are not equally easy to find, and that gradient is what a practitioner needs.} Four answer an
ordinary single-request capability check. Three are invisible to any check that sends one call per turn and
require a conversational state assembled by hand. One appears only while the model is generating. A
pre-flight built from single requests therefore does not cover the class, and a harness certified healthy
can still lose a cell after the money is spent --- which is why the last two fields of
Table~\ref{tab:checklist} ask for the models an endpoint refused and the message it refused them with.
Where each mechanism is filed in an existing taxonomy changes none of this: what the census establishes is
that several such mechanisms exist, that they act at different points of the evaluation lifecycle, that a
removed pair leaves no row to correct, and that they demand different detection.

A property of the apparatus usually assumed turned out to be measurable and to vary: temperature zero is
not determinism, with one model returning identical runs on 91--100\% of binaries and another on 20--24\%.
That output distributions can differ between services at one model identity is already
established~\citep{gao2025equality}; in one comparison of eight the difference here reaches the stability
of the score itself, 24.4\% of tasks against 66.7\% --- an existence result, not a claim about clouds in
general.

The pre-registered family answers the magnitude question at the resolution the design reached. Four models,
two managed clouds and two tool transports were run against hypotheses and an analysis script frozen
together before any data existed, with two of the ten tests implemented under a timestamped amendment
(\S\ref{sec:method}). No test passes its Holm threshold, and two sensitivity series computed afterwards
agree. What the family adds is the resolution itself: across eight contrasts of one design, the effect each
could separate spans a factor of 23.7, so what an experiment of this kind can resolve is a property of the
cell and not of the study. On the two contrasts with the largest between-task dispersion the lever that binds is
the number of tasks rather than the number of runs --- 0.5\% run-to-run noise and a floor of 668 binaries
--- so a budget sized on a borrowed calibration goes to the axis that does not bind. That is a statement
about what a design can resolve, and it is separate from the family's outcome on effect sizes.

\textbf{The transport moves a number, which is a bias. The endpoint deletes a row, which is a selection.}
The first is bounded, correctable, and already visible in a public leaderboard; the second is not, because
the model it refused has no line to correct. Existing reporting guidance does not make all of the apparatus
fields examined here equally explicit. Declaring the transport, the endpoint, and the models each endpoint
refused costs three lines in a method section, and without them a published agentic number does not
identify the system it describes.
