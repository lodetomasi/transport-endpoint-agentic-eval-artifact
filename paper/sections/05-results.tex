\section{Analysis and Interpretation}
\label{sec:results}

The census establishes that an endpoint can remove a model before a score exists. This section asks how
far the surviving numbers move, and reports beside each answer what that contrast could have separated.

All numbers here come from the isolated-workspace re-collection: an independent execution of the full
$4\times2\times2$ design on workspaces isolated per cell, comprising 5{,}809 valid measurements. It carries
the primary analysis because its apparatus removes the shared-workspace mechanism of \S\ref{sec:threats}
by construction, which is the reason that does not depend on either batch's numbers. The original
collection is reported beside it throughout, and the four concordance diagnostics are all met
(\S\ref{sec:threats}). Those diagnostics corroborate rather than validate blind: their thresholds were
fixed with the original collection's outcome already known, and Appendix~\ref{sec:appendice-provenienza}
states when each was written and against what.

\subsection{Which lever binds is a property of the cell}
\label{sec:varianza}

The variance of a paired difference decomposes into within-binary noise, which shrinks with more runs per
binary, and heterogeneity between binaries, which shrinks only with more binaries. The two are not
interchangeable, and Table~\ref{tab:variance} shows the choice is not a property of the axis: for T3 and T6
more runs buy nothing, at 668 and 380 binaries against the 45 collected.

\input{tables/variance}

These are estimates on 45 units. Bootstrapping the binaries --- the unit the contrast is paired on, 5{,}000
replications at a declared seed --- gives T3's noise share as 0.5\% $[0.0, 1.4]$ with $K_\infty$ 668 $[265,
1136]$, and T6's as 9.1\% $[4.1, 21.6]$ with 380 $[126, 744]$. The interval on $K_\infty$ is wide and its
lower end is still six times the 45 binaries collected, so the conclusion that more runs buy nothing does
not rest on the point estimate; neither noise-share interval reaches the 50\% that would make the two
components indistinguishable.

\textbf{On two contrasts the components are poorly identified at the boundary, and we report none rather
than a precise zero.} The rows marked $\dagger$ have estimated noise exceeding the observed variance. That
is not a finding about those cells but a variance model that does not fit them: the decomposition assumes
the two conditions' runs are independent given the binary and clamps the residual at zero when that fails.
The bootstrap makes the failure countable: the residual is clamped in \textbf{86\%} of replications on T7,
69\% on T8 and 43\% on T5. \textbf{The rule applied is that a contrast whose residual is clamped in more than half of the bootstrap
replications carries no decomposition here}, and it was written after computing the bootstrap on these
eight contrasts rather than before: it separates T7 and T8 from the rest, and no conclusion of this paper
rests on either. On T1, T3 and T6 clamping occurs in at most 1\% of replications, which is why those three
are the ones the decomposition is read on.

The two excluded contrasts are also the only two whose intervals are narrow enough for a $\pm3$pp boundary
to fall outside them (Table~\ref{tab:tests}), and that is mechanical rather than coincidental: a cell with
very little observed variance yields both a narrow interval and a variance model that collapses at the
boundary.

\subsubsection*{What the metric's granularity does and does not explain}
\label{sec:granularita}

Our measurement is coarse: pass-rate over five unit tests gives a run \emph{six} values, 53\% of the
5{,}809 rows sit at an extreme and 33\% of the 720 binary-by-cell means land exactly on 0 or 1. A floor-
and ceiling-heavy measurement is on its own sufficient for a large between-binary spread, so it is a rival
account of Table~\ref{tab:variance}.

So it was measured on this corpus. If saturation produces the between-binary spread, removing the saturated
binaries should drive the noise share up. Two exclusion rules: dropping the binaries saturated in
\emph{both} cells removes the pure artifact, while dropping those saturated in \emph{either} cell also
discards binaries one arm solved and the other did not, which \emph{are} informative --- the severe rule
plays against the finding. Each share is resampled over the binaries of its own subset. On T3: 0.5\%
$[0.0, 1.4]$ over all 45 binaries, 0.7\% $[0.0, 2.0]$ over the 29 surviving the first rule, 1.7\% $[0.0,
3.2]$ over the 20 surviving the second. On T6: 9.1\% $[4.1, 21.6]$, then 15.6\% $[10.9, 25.0]$, then
17.0\% $[11.3, 32.6]$. \textbf{No upper bound reaches the half} that would make noise and heterogeneity
indistinguishable, under either rule.

Excluding binaries also reduces $K$, so the rise could be an effect of subset size. Removing $K$ binaries
\emph{at random} at each declared size, 20{,}000 times, places the observed share at the 78.7th percentile
of the random distribution for T3's first rule, the 96.0th for its second, the 96.5th for T6's first and
the 84.6th for its second. \textbf{Two of the four are individually significant at the conventional
level} --- the 96.0th and 96.5th percentiles, so about 4.0\% and 3.5\% of random subsets reach the observed
share --- and they are consistent with granularity contributing to the between-binary spread. The other two
are not: a random subset matches or exceeds them roughly one time in five. \textbf{Conceding that
contribution at its observed size changes nothing the decomposition needs}: even the largest rise, T6 from
9.1\% to 17.0\%, leaves the noise share far from the half at which noise and heterogeneity would be
indistinguishable. The consequence for a designer is in
\S\ref{sec:proposizioni}: size the tests per task the way the tasks are sized, and report the floor and
ceiling shares beside any decomposition.

\subsection{RQ6 \emph{(exploratory, arose during execution)}: the calibration this design was
sized on, and what it can be compared against}
\label{sec:rq6}

The pre-registration inherited a calibration of 84\% run-to-run noise and $K{=}13$, and this design was
sized on it. \textbf{Measured on this apparatus, the binding lever is the number of tasks}: 0.5\% noise and
a floor of 668 binaries on the contrast with the largest observed dispersion, 9.1\% and 380 on the second
such contrast. The inherited figure comes from a batch whose validity flag had failed, and independent
reconstructions of it disagree, so what stands is the inversion measured here rather than a comparison
between the two numbers.

\textbf{The consequence is about where a budget goes, not how large it must be.} Reaching the floor this
contrast implies would take 668 binaries at eight runs on both arms --- 10{,}688 trials, roughly \$253 at
the prices its own cells paid, against the \$17 those cells cost. On contrasts with this much between-task
heterogeneity, additional budget buys more from task diversity than from repeated runs of the same tasks,
and the two are not interchangeable at any budget. A design sized on 84\%/$K{=}13$ buys the second.

The two collections agree with each other --- 0.1\%/705 against 0.5\%/668, and 9.3\%/487 against 9.1\%/380
--- which is \textbf{internal reproducibility}. Establishing that a variance component varies \emph{between}
apparatuses takes two apparatuses, so that question is carried to \S\ref{sec:proposizioni} as a
proposition.

\subsection{RQ1--RQ3 \emph{(confirmatory)}: a negative pre-registered result, and the resolution at
which it was established}

This subsection reports a \emph{negative} pre-registered result and reports it as such: the hypotheses,
the test family and the analysis were fixed before the data existed (\S\ref{sec:method}), the family
resolves no effect, and the outcome is stated before the exploratory findings that did produce something.
What it contributes is not the absence of an effect but the \emph{resolution} at which the absence was
established, which differs by a factor of 23.7 between contrasts sharing one design.

Of the ten tests fixed in the pre-registration, \emph{none} passes its Holm threshold at
$m=10$~\citep{holm1979}. The smallest $p$ the frozen script emits is $0.0155$, against a threshold of
$0.0050$. Table~\ref{tab:tests} reports that frozen series and prints each test's own threshold beside it;
two sensitivity series computed after the data existed --- the exact Student and a distribution-free
sign-flip --- give the same family outcome and are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendice-permutazione}.

The family's fixed composition costs something arithmetically, and the amount is computable. Two of the
ten tests are not independent of the other eight, so Holm at $m=10$ divides the smallest threshold by ten
rather than by eight --- $0.0050$ instead of $0.0063$. The smallest $p$ in the family, $0.0155$, is above
both, so the outcome does not turn on that choice; on a study whose smallest $p$ fell between them it
would. $m=10$ stands because the pre-registration fixed it and because it is the conservative direction, not as
the only defensible count: correcting RQ1--RQ2 and RQ3 as separate families would also be defensible, and
on these data it changes nothing, since the smallest $p$ clears no threshold under any of those countings.
The artifact carries the series.

That answers RQ1--RQ3 in the direction of ``not at this resolution'', and the resolution is the
reportable quantity: an evaluation that cannot separate 4pp from zero is not free to call two transports
equivalent.

\textbf{The pre-specified threshold and the resolvable threshold are two different quantities, and this
design separates them.} The pre-registration fixed $\pm 3$pp as the band outside which an effect would
falsify the null, and fixed it before any dispersion had been observed. The dispersions that arrived span
a factor of 23.7 (Table~\ref{tab:variance}), and the intervals of Table~\ref{tab:tests} are their direct
consequence: \textbf{on six of the eight contrasts the interval is too wide for a $\pm3$pp boundary to
fall outside it}, and on five it is wider than the 4.87pp the design was sized for. \textbf{The lesson is about sizing, not about pre-registration:} a
pre-specified falsifier threshold is only usable if the planned design can resolve it, and that
compatibility is checkable at design time from a variance estimate on the same apparatus
(\S\ref{sec:rq6}). The frozen $\pm3$pp boundary is accordingly read here as a pre-specified threshold and
not as evidence about the contrasts whose dispersion sits above it. That sample sizes in this field are
often set without such a check is not new: 103 software engineering experiments were found systematically
below accepted norms~\citep{dyba2006power}, a decade after the concern was raised as ``missing and
misunderstood''~\citep{miller1997power}, and it has been documented again for language-model
evaluation~\citep{card2020power}.

Table~\ref{tab:tests} and Fig.~\ref{fig:effects} are the whole confirmatory result. T3 reproduces the
transport difference that motivated this study at $-9.83$pp --- different apparatus, isolated workspaces,
three information leaks closed (\S\ref{sec:threats}) --- and T6 measures $7.94$pp between two deployed
services serving the same nominal model at fixed transport. Neither passes its Holm threshold, and both
are contrasts whose observed dispersion is among the largest in the study. All three statements hold
together.

\subsection{RQ1--RQ3 \emph{(confirmatory)}: what a contrast could separate is a property of the cell}

The pre-registration sized the design with a pooled standard deviation of $0.1062$ and a conservative
$0.1167$, both calibrated on the companion study, giving one minimum detectable effect of 4.87pp for every
cell. The observed standard deviations of the paired differences span $0.0117$ to $0.2774$, \textbf{a ratio
of 23.7}, and the interval widths of Table~\ref{tab:tests} are the same fact in the units of the effect:
they are the standard deviations rescaled by a constant, so the heterogeneity is readable off either
column and needs no third. What it means is that a design sized on a single band quotes one number for
eight contrasts that differ by more than an order of magnitude in what they can separate --- which is a
property of the cells, not of the study, and is the reason \S\ref{sec:rq6} treats the sizing axis as an
empirical question.

A null result here therefore reads: no contrast places its effect outside the band its own dispersion
permits. It does \emph{not} read as equivalence --- $K=45$ does not support an equivalence test at 3pp,
which would need 99--119 binaries, and this was declared before the data existed.

\subsection{RQ5 \emph{(exploratory, arose during execution)}: how much of the transport effect call
batching could account for}
\label{sec:rq5}

The pre-registered transport contrast moves two things at once on the models that batch: the format of
the call and the number a turn admits --- haiku issues 1.438 calls per turn against sonnet's 1.333. A
declared confound is not a bounded one, so the arm that separates them was collected: native transport
forced to \emph{one call per turn}, all else held fixed. It is exploratory, does not enter the family of
ten, and its stopping criterion was fixed before it ran.

Removing batching does not lower the score: both point estimates are \emph{positive} and both intervals
contain zero (Table~\ref{tab:batching}), against a transport effect of $-9.83$pp on the same model and
endpoint. \textbf{The arm bounds rather than eliminates, and the bound is arithmetic.} Read at the most
unfavourable end of each interval --- $-2.6$pp on haiku, $-1.3$pp on sonnet --- batching would account for
at most \textbf{26\%} and \textbf{13\%} of the $-9.83$pp. That is what the arm supports: not that
batching contributes nothing, but that it is not the principal account, with a ceiling attached.

\subsection{RQ5, second finding \emph{(exploratory)}: one refusal reconfigures the model
for the rest of the run}
\label{sec:rq5b}

The arm produced a second result. Across 1{,}084 trajectories \emph{every} first turn
issues more than one call and is constrained; after the first refusal the rate collapses to 0.2\%, eight
turns out of 4{,}457. The model does not learn the constraint from the tool schema, which does not encode
it --- it learns it from one rejection, and the lesson holds for the rest of the trajectory.

\textbf{The arm falls outside its pre-declared validity band, and that governs what it licenses.} The band
required 33--38\% of turns to carry a constrained call, estimated from the unconstrained batching rate; the
observed figure is 19.7\%. Under the rule fixed in \S\ref{sec:design} the arm therefore licenses no
statement about the \emph{size} of the batching effect, and none is made. The reason the band was missed is
itself the measurement above: it assumed a batching rate constant along the trajectory, whereas the collapse
fixes the achievable share at essentially one constrained turn per trajectory. Of the 5{,}541 turns carrying
a call, 1{,}084 are first turns and all are constrained, and eight more are constrained later --- 1{,}092 in
total, \textbf{19.7\%}, which is the structural ceiling of 19.6\% plus those eight. The band's 33--38\% was
unreachable under the constraint, so its premise was wrong rather than the data.

\emph{The arm therefore licenses the ceiling above and no point estimate of the batching effect}, and the
ceiling needs only the two intervals rather than the band that was missed. It also explains an asymmetry in
the main comparison --- under the prompted-text protocol the option to batch never exists, while under
constrained native it exists for one turn and the model abandons it after one refusal.

\textbf{The input-token divergence is a provider-reported accounting difference, and it decomposes.} Per
run, the two services report a ratio of $0.43$. Two components of that are visible in the released traces.
The first is trajectory length: the median run takes six turns on one service and four on the other, so
more history accumulates before the run ends. The second is what the counters report at the \emph{first}
turn, where no history exists yet and the client sends the same system prompt, the same four tool schemas
and the same initial message: there the reported counts are 1{,}263 against 678, a ratio of $0.54$. \textbf{Two readings of that ratio
are not separated by the released evidence}: the two stacks may serialise the same content differently ---
the tool schemas travel in each provider's own format --- or they may count the same payload differently.
Both are figures the providers report, and the released traces record exactly that: per-turn provider
usage. \textbf{The quantity is therefore a provider-reported input-token accounting difference}, which is
what the two services disagree about, and the paper uses it no further.

\textbf{The source of T6's score difference is not identified here.} Output throughput agrees to within 6\%
while several other quantities do not, and that pattern is consistent with more than one account; naming
one would be an inference the design does not license. What stands is narrower and sufficient for the
paper's argument: the two deployed services differ in trajectory length, in latency and in reported context
volume, at fixed nominal model identity, so \textbf{T6 is a difference between deployed services} and no
design that names only the model can attribute it further. That is the same form the transport axis takes
in \S\ref{sec:threats}. The companion study identified context volume as a free parameter in its own
right~\citep{companion2026baseline}; here it is one of several quantities that move together with the
factor being manipulated, and Table~\ref{tab:tests} should be read with that attached to T6.

\subsubsection*{The turn budget, measured rather than assumed}
\label{sec:budget-turni}

Because Table~\ref{tab:runtime} raises it, the budget is reported across the whole design rather than for one
cell. The re-collection produced 5{,}880 trajectories, of which 5{,}809 are valid measurements by the
predicate of \S\ref{sec:method}. Counting trajectories, \textbf{68 reach the turn budget --- 1.16\%} --- and
all 68 are in the native arm; no textual-transport trajectory reaches it. The saturation is localised
rather than widespread: it is concentrated in the two \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} native cells, at 10.0\% on
Databricks and 6.3\% on Bedrock, and is at or below 0.3\% in the other fourteen. Median turns used per cell
range from 3 to 6 against a budget of 12. Two consequences follow and neither is speculative. Every effect
reported here is conditional on this fixed budget, and the budget binds asymmetrically: it binds only where
a model can batch calls, which is the arm this study manipulates. The turn budget and the termination
condition are therefore fields in Table~\ref{tab:checklist}, not footnotes.

\subsection{Temperature zero is not determinism, and one demonstrated case where it differs by service}

Two properties of the apparatus are usually assumed and turn out to be measurable. \emph{First,
temperature zero is not determinism.} All cells ran at temperature $0.0$, and across the eight runs of the
same binary in the same cell haiku returns an identical pass-rate on 91--100\% of binaries against
gpt-oss's 20--24\%, with 56\% of binaries over the study yielding eight identical runs
(Fig.~\ref{fig:determinism}).

Second, \textbf{one comparison of eight demonstrates that this stability can differ between two deployed
services at fixed nominal model identity.} On 45 binaries the sampling separates in exactly one case: llama
on the native transport returns identical scores on 24.4\% of binaries at one endpoint $[14.2, 38.7]$ and
66.7\% at the other $[52.1, 78.6]$, Wilson bands not touching --- and they remain disjoint at the
Bonferroni-corrected level for eight comparisons, $[11.5, 44.7]$ against $[46.3, 82.2]$. The other seven
overlap and are not used as evidence. \textbf{This is an existence demonstration and it is not read here as a
population claim}: it establishes that score stability need not be a property of the model identity alone,
not that stability generally differs between clouds, and one case out of eight cannot support the second.
The comparison falls on the same model and axis where T6 shows the largest endpoint effect in the study,
and the original collection isolates the same comparison.

\subsection{What the metric buys, measured}

Pass-rate has a floor reachable without reconstructing anything, and it was measured rather than
assumed: the 53 runs that called \emph{no} tool average $0.1132$ against $0.6378$ for the 5{,}756
that called at least one. The floor exists and does not dominate --- the metric is a lower bound with a
measured floor, and the artifact carries the distribution.
\subsection{Declared covariates}

Two confounds were measured before the confirmatory numbers were read, and neither is corrected for: doing
so after the fact is the freedom pre-registration exists to remove. They are calls per turn, which the
ablation arm of \S\ref{sec:rq5} isolates and which binds on two models of four, and material acquired,
where the native arm asks for more functions per binary in all eight cells. The deposit carries both with
their per-cell figures.

\subsection{Scope}

The effect sizes are circumscribed twice over and both bounds are part of the result. They come from
\emph{one} family of tasks, decompiled C binaries scored by unit tests, on four models and two clouds,
so T3's $-9.83$pp is a measurement on this corpus rather than a quantity transferable elsewhere: a reader
who needs a number for their own setting should take the method and the variance decomposition, not the
point estimate. \textbf{And every empirical statement above concerns \emph{our prompted-text protocol}} ---
one call per turn, on the last line of the response --- \textbf{rather than textual tool calling in
general}, about which a leaderboard reporting both modes finds the prompted variant \emph{ahead} by 6pp on
one model, against the direction that seeded this work. Nothing here is evidence about prompted-text
protocols that batch calls, that use a different delimiter, or that return results in a different role.
